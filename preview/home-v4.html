Upcore frameworks

The thinking behind every engagement.

Four working models behind every engagement, each grounded in established research. They govern how agents behave, how much autonomy they earn, what we automate and where the time comes back.

Computer science

Agent Control Loop

Every Upcore agent runs the same closed loop: sense what changed, reason against your rules, act in your systems, verify the result and learn from the outcome.

When confidence falls below the threshold you set, the loop stops at a human gate instead of guessing.

  • Grounded in your SOPs and data, not general knowledge
  • Every action verified and written to an audit log
  • Low-confidence decisions routed to a named person

Adapted from the autonomic-computing control loop (monitor, analyse, plan, execute over shared knowledge): Kephart & Chess, “The Vision of Autonomic Computing”, IEEE Computer, 2003.

Behavioural science

Autonomy Ladder

Trust in automation should be earned, not assumed. Too little and people redo the agent’s work; too much and errors slip through.

Every agent starts at Level 2, drafting work for a person to approve. It moves up only when its measured accuracy on your real cases justifies it, and you approve every promotion.

  • Five levels, from manual to autonomous with sampled audit
  • Promotion on measured accuracy, never by default
  • Any agent can be stepped back down at any time

Derived from: Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens, “A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation”, IEEE Trans. SMC-A, 2000; Lee & See, “Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance”, Human Factors, 2004.

Strategy

Automation Fit Matrix

Not every process should be automated, and not in the same way. We score each workflow on two axes: how much volume it carries and how rule-based its decisions are.

Where a process lands decides how we automate it: end to end, as a draft for a person to approve, on a schedule, or not at all.

  • Scored in discovery against your real volumes
  • High-volume, rule-based work goes first, because it pays back fastest
  • Judgment-heavy work gets an agent that drafts, never one that decides

Adapted from the product–process matrix: Hayes & Wheelwright, “Link Manufacturing Process and Product Life Cycles”, Harvard Business Review, 1979.

Operations science

Lead-time Compression

Most process time is not work, it is waiting: for a reply, a document, a signature, a bank. Agents don’t make your people work faster; they remove the waiting between the steps.

That is where weeks come back. For one residential developer, time from booking to first installment fell from 6–10 weeks to about 3.

  • Work stays with people; chasing goes to agents
  • Measured end to end, not task by task
  • Less work in progress means faster cash and fewer status calls

Grounded in Little’s Law: J. D. C. Little, “A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW”, Operations Research, 1961.

FAQs

Questions engineering leaders ask us first.

Anything else? A 45-minute discovery call with a certified architect is the fastest way to find out.

How is this different from giving developers Copilot or Claude?

Those tools generate code. AI-native engineering is the delivery process around them: the spec template, the architecture guardrails, the plan gate, the automated pull-request gates, the merge rules, the eval harness, the controlled release and the decision record. Without that process, AI-written code still depends on manual review to be trusted.

Do we have to replace our tools?

No. We install the process inside what you already run: Jira or Linear, GitHub, your existing CI/CD and the AI assistants you have approved, such as Claude, GitHub Copilot or Microsoft Power Platform for low-code teams.

Who decides when AI-written code can merge?

You do. You set the merge thresholds. Only low-risk changes that conform to your architecture rules auto-merge; everything above the threshold goes to a named approver, and every decision is recorded on the dashboard.

How do you handle security and intellectual property?

Code stays in your repositories and builds run in your environments. Security scanning is a mandatory gate, builds run in sandboxes, and we configure AI providers so your code is not used to train their models. Upcore is ISO 27001 certified and delivers to CMMI Level 3 processes.

How does a pilot work?

We pick one team and one service with a real backlog, agree success measures against your current process up front, install the pipeline end to end, and review the results with you before any wider rollout.

Do you also automate business operations?

Yes. Once the engineering pipeline is in place, many clients use the same governed approach for operations: AI agents for follow-ups, documents, reconciliations and customer updates, with people approving what matters. See Business Process Automation.
